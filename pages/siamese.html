History of the Siamese cat

The story of the Siamese cat, from its origins in Siam (now Thailand) to homes around the world.

A cat from Siam

The Siamese comes from Siam, the country now known as Thailand. Cats like it appear in old Thai manuscripts, and it has long been associated with its homeland.

Arriving in Britain

Siamese cats reached Britain in the 1880s and quickly caught the public's imagination. Their unusual colouring and striking blue eyes were unlike the cats people were used to, and the breed became a favourite at cat shows.

Appearance

The Siamese has a "pointed" coat: a pale body with darker colour on the ears, face, paws and tail. This pattern is linked to temperature, with the cooler parts of the body growing darker fur. Kittens are born pale and darken as they grow. Almond-shaped blue eyes complete the look.

Temperament

Siamese cats are known for being sociable, curious and very vocal. Many bond closely with their people and enjoy company and play.

Modern and traditional types

Over the years, show breeding produced the modern Siamese, which is slender with a long body, a wedge-shaped head and large ears. The traditional Siamese, also called the Thai cat by some, keeps a rounder head and a sturdier build, closer to the cats first seen in the West.
