History of the French Bulldog

How the French Bulldog grew from a small English toy bulldog into a favourite city companion.

English roots

The French Bulldog descends from small bulldogs kept in England, often called toy bulldogs. They were kept as companions and were popular in the lace-making towns of the English Midlands, including Nottingham.

Across the Channel

During the 19th century, many lace workers lost work to machines and moved to France, taking their small bulldogs with them. In France the dogs were bred with other local dogs and the breed took on its own look, most famously the upright "bat" ears.

Paris takes notice

The little dogs became fashionable in Paris, where they were popular with a wide mix of people, from shopkeepers to artists and society figures. The name "French Bulldog" stuck, and the breed soon travelled again, this time to America.

Popularity in the United States

American fans took to the breed, and the bat ear became the accepted look there. In recent years the French Bulldog has ranked among the most popular breeds in the United States.

Good to know

The flat face that makes the breed so recognisable can also cause breathing difficulties, so thoughtful breeding and care matter. Always ask a vet or a responsible breeder about health before bringing one home.
